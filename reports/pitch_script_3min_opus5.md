# Track 2 pitch — 3-minute script

**Target: 2:55.** Spoken text below is ~440 words at ~150 wpm. Stage directions in *italics*.
Read it straight — no intro music, no title card longer than 3 seconds. Judges are watching dozens of these.

---

### 0:00 – 0:18 · Open on the answer, not on yourself

*Slide 1: the two variants, large, nothing else.*

> This child has Mosaic Variegated Aneuploidy type 1. Two variants in *BUB1B* —
> a stop at codon 737 that ClinVar already labels pathogenic for this exact disease,
> and a novel missense at asparagine 1002 — one allele in one and a half million —
> sitting in the pseudokinase domain.
>
> We found it ranked first genome-wide, with no gene-panel prior, and we confirmed
> it from the raw reads without trusting the variant caller.

### 0:18 – 0:50 · The mechanism, in one idea

*Slide 2: null allele → 0 protein. Hypomorph → 5–10%. One arrow.*

> The truncating allele makes no protein — it's degraded by nonsense-mediated decay.
> So everything this child has comes from the other copy. And the published work on
> that class of mutation is unusually clear: these are not broken proteins. They are
> *scarce* proteins. Same messenger RNA, five to ten times less protein, twice the
> turnover — and when you force them back up to normal levels, the checkpoint works
> perfectly again.
>
> So this is not a disease of a protein that doesn't function. It's a disease of a
> protein that doesn't last.

### 0:50 – 1:30 · The number

*Slide 3: the dosage figure. Let it sit. Don't talk over the reveal.*

> That reframes the target, and it lets us put a number on it.
>
> The dose–response for BUBR1 is a switch, not a slope. Six percent of normal and
> most cells missegregate. Thirteen percent and segregation is essentially fine.
>
> This child sits between five and ten percent. Right on the edge.
>
> To get him over the line, you need to raise one destabilised protein by
> **one-point-three to two-point-six fold.** Not restore it. Not replace the gene.
> Roughly double it.
>
> That is a drug-scale target. And it means every candidate we test has a
> pre-registered bar to clear, decided before we run anything.

### 1:30 – 2:20 · The inversion — the part they won't have heard

*Slide 4: two columns, "cancer" and "MVA", same drug list, opposite verdicts.*

> Now the part we think matters most.
>
> Every published aneuploidy-selective drug — the HSP90 inhibitors, the AMPK
> activators, chloroquine — works by killing aneuploid cells preferentially. In a
> tumour sitting inside a healthy patient, that difference is the whole therapy.
>
> But in this disease the *patient* is the aneuploid organism. His brain, his muscle,
> his marrow all carry it. The therapeutic index isn't narrow. It's **inverted.**
>
> So that entire literature is a list of things to avoid here, not a list of leads.
> And for this child specifically, HSP90 inhibitors are the worst of them — because
> his surviving allele is the one that depends on HSP90 to fold. You would be
> stripping away the only working copy he has.
>
> The same logic rules out senolytics, and growth hormone, and it raises a question
> nobody has asked about his current treatment.

### 2:20 – 2:45 · The clinical hook

*Slide 5: one line — "Vincristine needs the checkpoint. He doesn't have one."*

> He has rhabdomyosarcoma, and rhabdomyosarcoma is treated with vincristine.
>
> Vinca alkaloids don't kill by damaging microtubules. They kill by holding a cell in
> mitotic arrest — an arrest maintained by the spindle checkpoint. His checkpoint is
> the disease. His cells slip out of arrest in about an hour where normal cells hold
> indefinitely.
>
> So the prediction is that they don't die. They escape, carrying a wrecked genome.
> In a child with a thirty-seven percent lifetime cancer risk, that's a serious question.
>
> One plate of patient cells answers it.

### 2:45 – 2:55 · Close

*Slide 6: the three-line experiment. Hold on screen through the end.*

> One cell system, three readouts, one pre-registered threshold.
> Protein up one-point-three fold or it isn't a hit.
>
> Everything is public, and it runs on one CPU. Thank you.

## Production notes

- **Six slides, no more.** Static. No transitions. Screen-record with the script on a second monitor.
- **Do not read the caveats aloud.** They belong in the written report, which the judges have.
  Saying "this is not medical advice" on camera costs eight seconds and adds nothing — it's already
  on slide 1 as small print and in the report.
- **The reveal is slide 3.** Pause a full second after "Right on the edge" before giving the number.
- **Say "one-point-three to two-point-six", not "1.3 to 2.6×"** — it scans better spoken.
- If you overrun, cut 0:18–0:50 down to two sentences. Never cut the vincristine section.
- Upload unlisted to YouTube; the form only needs the URL.
